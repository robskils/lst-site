If you are moving 60 wedding guests from a Lisbon hotel to a venue in Sintra and back, the short answer is this. Split them across two or more vehicles rather than relying on one big coach. Leave Lisbon with well over an hour in hand. Plan at least two return departures after the party, not one. The return is where most wedding transport goes wrong, usually because it was planned last.

I'm Robin. I run Lisbon Sintra Tours, and I have driven and guided the road between Lisbon and Sintra for years, in daylight and at two in the morning. Here is how I would think it through for a group of that size.

What size of vehicle for 60 guests

On paper, 60 people fit into one large coach with a smaller vehicle alongside, or into two mid-size coaches. Which is right depends less on the head count and more on the venue.

Many Sintra venues sit in the hills, reached by narrow, winding lanes with tight gates and sharp turns. A full-size coach may not get to the door. That can mean dropping guests at the end of a lane and asking them to walk uphill in wedding shoes, and then walk back down in the dark. Nobody remembers the ceremony fondly after that.

Before I quote, I want the venue's exact address. Ideally I also want a word with the venue about where vehicles can stop and turn. If a big coach cannot reach the entrance, two or three smaller vehicles often can.

Splitting the group has a second advantage: it gives you flexibility on the way home, which matters more than most couples expect. Seat counts vary from vehicle to vehicle, so I quote with the exact seats in each. I would also leave a few spare, because head counts move in the last fortnight.

Timing the outbound journey

Allow a full hour between Lisbon and Sintra, whichever end you start from. That is driving time. It does not include loading 60 people in formal clothes outside a hotel, which always takes longer than it looks. If guests are spread across more than one hotel, each extra stop adds time too.

Work backwards from the ceremony. Guests should arrive with time to find their seats, not walk in behind the bride. Set the pickup from there. Put the departure time in the guest information and ask people to be in the lobby ten minutes before it.

It also helps to name one person at each hotel, a friend or relative with the guest list, who knows which vehicle is which and can tell the driver when everyone is aboard.

The return nobody plans

This is the part I would spend most of your planning time on.

  • Not everyone leaves at once. Older relatives, parents with small children and anyone on an early flight will want to go well before the dancing stops. If there is a single departure at one in the morning, they either sit it out exhausted or try to find their own way back. A sensible plan has an earlier run and a later one at the very least.
  • Drivers have limits on their working day. A vehicle that brings guests out in the afternoon and waits until after midnight means a very long shift for one driver. Rest rules restrict how long a driver can be on duty, so a late return often needs a different driver or a vehicle that comes out fresh in the evening. This affects the cost, and it is far better to know that when you are budgeting than to find out later.
  • Do not count on taxis as a backup. Late at night, from a venue up in the Sintra hills, a taxi or ride-hailing car can be slow to arrive or not come at all. I would not plan around one.
  • Somebody should own the last departure. Name a person at the venue who knows when each vehicle leaves and checks it against the list, so nobody is left behind because they were saying goodbye to an aunt.
  • Agree what happens if the party runs late. Decide in advance how much the final departure can slip, and what that costs, so nobody is negotiating with a driver at midnight.

If guests are going back to several hotels, settle the drop-off order beforehand. At that hour, a short route matters to tired people.

Wedding guest transport in Lisbon: what to decide before you ask for a quote

You do not need everything final, but these make the difference between a rough figure and a plan that works:

  • The venue's exact address and anything they tell you about vehicle access
  • Which hotels guests are staying in, and roughly how many at each
  • Ceremony time and the time the venue closes
  • How many guests travel each way, which is not always the same number
  • How many return departures you want, and roughly when
  • Whether any guests need step-free access or extra help boarding

If guests are making a few days of it

Plenty of guests turn a wedding into a short break, and some couples arrange a group day out before or after. A few things are worth knowing early. Pena Palace and Quinta da Regaleira are timed entry and sell out, so book once your numbers are reasonably firm. The road up to Pena will not take a full-size coach, so a large group needs more, smaller vehicles for that day as well. Jerónimos Monastery and Belém Tower are closed on Mondays, which catches people out when the day after a weekend wedding is a Monday. I quote entry tickets separately from transport.

Sending me your details

If you have a date and a rough head count, send them to me with the venue address and the hotels your guests are using. I'll come back with a quote and a suggested plan for both directions, including the return. The numbers do not need to be final to start the conversation.
