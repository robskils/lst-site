Groups of ten to two hundred

The river takes the whole party at once.

On land a private day steps - at seven people, at fifteen, at twenty-two - and each step adds a vehicle and a guide. On the water it does not step. One deck, one group, and for the first time all week everybody is in the same room.

The short answer

Why a boat solves what a coach cannot.

The hardest part of a large group on land is not the cost. It is the splitting. Above twenty-two guests you are into a second coach, and a second coach needs a second guide, and from that moment the party is two parties having two slightly different days.

A boat does not work that way. Forty people and ninety people are the same arrangement on a bigger vessel: one deck, one crew, one conversation. Nobody is in the other vehicle. Nobody misses the toast.

That is why a charter is often the right answer for the one evening of a trip that everybody is meant to remember - the welcome, the last night, the reason the group came together in the first place.

The boat

We do not keep a fleet. We charter the right one.

Owning boats would mean selling you the boat we own. Instead we charter against the party: the number, the hours, the river and what the evening is for. What follows is the shape of it rather than a catalogue.

Ten to twenty

A sailing yacht

Quiet, close to the water, and the one size where guests can take the helm. Best for a small party that wants the river itself rather than a room with a view of it.

Twenty to sixty

A catamaran or a motor yacht

Flat, stable decks and room to stand in groups rather than sit in rows. This is the size most corporate evenings and most wedding parties land on.

Sixty to two hundred

A larger vessel

More than one deck, an indoor saloon for weather, proper lavatories and a galley that can serve a seated dinner rather than a tray of canapes.

Two rivers

The Tagus, and the one most visitors never see.

Lisbon's river is the obvious one, and it earns it. From the water the city finally makes sense as a whole: Belem Tower at the waterline, the Discoveries Monument, the 25 de Abril bridge going copper at the end of the day, the whole hill of the city laid out in one view you cannot get from any street.

An hour south, the Sado estuary at Setubal is a different proposition altogether - a resident pod of bottlenose dolphins, the Arrabida cliffs dropping into clear water, and almost none of the river traffic. For a group that has already done Lisbon, it is the day that surprises them.

Worth knowing early

Three things that decide a day on the water.

The afternoon wind

The Tagus picks up a northerly through the afternoon in summer and drops again towards evening. It is why the good charters sail late, and why an early-afternoon departure is the one we will usually talk you out of.

Boarding takes longer than you think

Getting a hundred and twenty guests down a pontoon and aboard is twenty minutes, not five, and it comes out of your charter not out of the schedule. We build it in and tell you the real time on the water.

Food is a decision, not an extra

Drinks and something to eat on deck is one kind of evening. A seated dinner for eighty is another, and it narrows the list of boats considerably. Worth settling before anything is held.

What it is usually for

The evening the trip is remembered by.

Conference groups who have spent three days in a windowless room. Incentive trips that need one night that is not a dinner. Wedding parties the evening before, when the guests have arrived and nothing has started yet. Birthdays with a number on them.

And, at the smaller end, families who want the Lisbon everyone photographs without the Lisbon everyone queues for.

It also folds neatly into a longer stay: a day in Sintra, a day in the city, and the boat on the evening in between.
