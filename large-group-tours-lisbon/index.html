Groups of 7 and up

Large private group tours, run properly.

Twenty people is not two groups of ten. Past the size of a single vehicle a private day becomes a different exercise - and there is a constraint at the top of the hill in Sintra that almost nobody mentions until it is too late to change it.

The short answer

What changes above a dozen people.

Up to seven, a private tour is a car and a guide who drives it. That is one person, one conversation, one set of decisions. It is also what most of the private tour market is built around.

Above that, three things change at once. The vehicle becomes a van, then a midi-coach, then more than one of them. Every vehicle needs its own guide, because a guide cannot be in two coaches at once. And the day itself has to be planned around what a larger vehicle can physically do - which roads it fits on, where it can set down, how long forty people take to get off and on.

None of that makes a large group harder to enjoy. It makes it harder to improvise. The work moves from the day itself to the fortnight before it.

The ladder

Party size decides the vehicles, and the vehicles decide the cost.

The price does not rise smoothly with the number of people. It steps, at the point where one more guest needs a second vehicle and a second guide. Twenty-two people and twenty-three people are a long way apart; twenty-three and forty-four are not.

The constraint nobody mentions

A full-size coach cannot reach Pena Palace.

The road up through the park is narrow, steep and has turns a long vehicle cannot take. Vehicles of about twenty-two seats and under go to the gate. Anything larger stops in the town below, and the group transfers up by shuttle - which works, and takes about forty minutes out of the day that nobody has budgeted for. We would rather you knew now than at ten past ten on the morning.

Before you book

Five questions worth asking any company.

  • How many vehicles, and how many guides? If the answer is one guide for two coaches, half your group spends the day without one.
  • What size vehicle, exactly? Ask for seats, not adjectives. "A coach" covers everything from nineteen seats to fifty-five, and only one of those gets up to Pena.
  • Are the palace tickets timed, and are they bought? Pena and Quinta da Regaleira sell out. A timed slot for forty is not something to leave until the week before.
  • Where does lunch happen? Most of the places a small group would love cannot seat a large one, and will not take a booking for it. A set menu arranged ahead is not a downgrade; it is the only thing that works.
  • What happens if six people drop out, or six more arrive? The honest answer is that the price may step. Ask where the steps are before you commit, not after.
The shape of a day

Where the eight hours actually go.

Between Lisbon and Sintra we allow a full hour each way, whichever end you start from. It is forty minutes on a quiet Tuesday in February and an hour and a quarter on a Saturday in May, and a plan built on the forty is a plan that runs late all day.

How we work

Seven and up, and nothing else.

We have made large private groups the whole of what we do. Smaller parties are passed straight to a trusted independent guide who looks after them directly - no mark-up, no middleman, and a better day than we could give them while thinking about a coach.

What that buys you: vehicles and guides we have run with for years, tickets booked in the right names at the right times, a plan written hour by hour before you arrive, and one person answering the phone on the day.

We are licensed, insured, APAVT members, and based in Lisbon. Portugal is not a destination we cover; it is where we live.
