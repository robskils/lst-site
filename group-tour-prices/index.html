Pricing, explained

What a day actually costs, and why.

Group tour pricing looks arbitrary from the outside. It is not - it is almost entirely decided by how many vehicles your party needs and how many guides have to be in them.

The principle

You are not buying seats. You are buying vehicles.

A per-head price makes sense for a scheduled bus tour, where you fill the empty seats with strangers. A private day has no strangers in it, so the cost of the vehicle is the same whether twelve people or twenty-two travel in it.

That is why the price per person falls as the group grows - right up until one more guest needs a second vehicle, at which point it jumps. Twenty-two and twenty-three people are a long way apart. Twenty-three and forty-four are almost the same day.

If you are close to one of those lines, it is worth knowing before you confirm numbers. We will always tell you where the nearest line is.

The ladder

Where the steps are.

Each step is a vehicle and the guide who has to be in it. Everything else - fuel, tolls, parking, the driver's day - scales with the vehicles too.

Worked examples

Three real shapes of day.

A full day of eight hours from Lisbon or Sintra, guided. Figures are indicative and move with the season, the date and what the day contains - but the shape of them does not change.

Note the middle row. Forty people often costs less per head than twenty-five, because twenty-five is the awkward number that needs two vehicles without filling either. Entry tickets are extra and quoted at cost: Pena Palace is €20 an adult, Quinta da Regaleira €20, Jéronimos €18.

The small print, said plainly

What is in the figure, and what is not.

  • In: the vehicles. Fuel, tolls, parking, insurance and the driver's hours, for as many vehicles as the party needs.
  • In: a guide in each one. In English - other languages on request and with notice.
  • In: the planning. An hour-by-hour plan before you arrive, tickets booked in the right names at the right times, and one person answering the phone on the day.
  • Not in: entry tickets. Quoted separately and at cost. They vary by site and by age band, and burying them in a day rate only makes the day rate look strange.
  • Not in: lunch. We arrange it - usually a set menu, which for a large group is the only thing that genuinely works - and it is billed at what it costs.
  • Not in: gratuities. Never expected, always passed on in full if offered.
