Can you take forty colleagues from Lisbon to Sintra and back in a day? Yes, and it can be a very good day. What decides whether it works is rarely the palaces themselves. It is where everyone gets picked up, how the group is split across vehicles, whether the timed entries are booked early enough, how forty people eat lunch without losing half the afternoon, and who is counting heads at each stop. I'm Robin, I run Lisbon Sintra Tours, and I've driven and guided these roads for years. These are the things I'd want an office manager to have settled before the day, not on it.

Planning a corporate day trip from Lisbon for a group of forty

A group of forty behaves differently from a family of four. Everything takes longer. Getting everyone off a vehicle, through a gate and back on again is a process, and any delay is multiplied by forty. The plan that works is one with fewer stops, realistic gaps between them and clear answers to a handful of practical questions. Most of those questions fall to whoever is organising, so it helps to know what they are.

Pick-up points: one place or several?

If your team is staying in one hotel, this is simple. If they are spread across the city, you have a choice to make. Collecting from several hotels feels considerate but adds time to the start of the day, and the first people picked up spend that time sitting and waiting. One or two agreed meeting points usually work better.

Two things are worth knowing. First, some streets in the older parts of Lisbon are narrow and hard to stop in, so the front door of a hotel is not always the most sensible place to meet. A nearby spot where vehicles can pull in safely is often quicker. Second, a fixed departure time matters more than the exact spot. Send it to everyone in writing the day before, with a phone number to call if they are running late.

Why forty people will not travel on one coach

This catches a lot of organisers out. The road up to Pena Palace will not take a full-size coach. If Pena is on your list, forty people need to travel in several smaller vehicles rather than one large one.

That sounds like a complication, but it can work in your favour. Smaller vehicles mean smaller groups, and smaller groups move through sites more easily, hear their guide properly and are easier to keep together. It does mean deciding how to split people up. Some organisers mix departments on purpose, others keep teams together. Either is fine, as long as it is decided before the morning and everyone knows which vehicle they are in.

Timings that hold up on the day

Allow a full hour between Lisbon and Sintra, whichever end you start from. Traffic varies, and it is better to arrive with time in hand than to rush a group of forty through a timed entry.

Pena Palace and Quinta da Regaleira both run on timed entry, and both sell out. For a group this size, the slots need booking as soon as your date and numbers are reasonably firm. Leaving it until the week before is how groups end up seeing one and not the other. Entry tickets are quoted separately from transport and guiding, so you can see clearly what each part costs.

If you are thinking of adding Belém to the day, check the calendar first. Jerónimos Monastery and Belém Tower are closed on Mondays. A Monday trip can still be a good one, but it should be built around Sintra rather than trying to include both.

My general advice is to choose two main visits, not four. A group that sees two places properly goes home happier than one that has been marched past four.

Lunch for forty

Lunch is where many group days lose an hour they did not plan to lose. Forty people ordering individually from a full menu takes a long time, and the bill at the end takes longer.

  • Book ahead. Very few places can seat forty without notice, especially in Sintra at busy times of year.
  • Agree a set menu. A short choice of dishes, decided in advance, keeps lunch to a sensible length.
  • Collect dietary needs early. Ask everyone when they confirm attendance, not on the morning. Vegetarian, vegan, allergies and anything else should be passed on well before the day.
  • Decide who pays and how. One bill settled by the company is far quicker than forty separate ones.

We can talk through lunch options when we put a quote together, depending on where your day is going and the kind of meal you have in mind.

Who herds them

Every group needs someone keeping count, and with forty people this is not a job for whoever happens to be nearest the door. Our guides and drivers look after the route, the sites and the timings, but it helps a great deal to have one named person from your side as well. Their job is simple: know who is meant to be there, know who has had to leave early, and be the person colleagues go to rather than wandering off to find the guide.

A few habits make this easier:

  • Give each vehicle a short list of its passengers.
  • Share one or two phone numbers with the whole group, written down, not just mentioned.
  • Agree a meeting point at each site and a time to be back there.
  • Decide in advance what happens if someone is late back: does the vehicle wait, or do they catch up?
  • Find out beforehand if anyone has limited mobility. Some of Sintra involves steep paths and steps, and it is better to plan around that than to discover it on the hill.

What to send me

If you are planning a group day out from Lisbon, the most useful things to know are your preferred dates, rough numbers, where people are staying, and which places matter most to you. If a Monday is on the table, mention it, as that changes what is possible. Send those over and I'll come back with a plan and a quote, with tickets shown separately, and we can adjust from there.
