If you are planning a day out of Lisbon for a group, you want to know what it will cost before you start writing emails. I do not publish one fixed price, because a quote for 20 people and a quote for 70 are built differently. The cost comes down to three things: how many vehicles you need, how many guides, and how many hours the day runs. Entry tickets are quoted separately. Below I explain how each of those changes as a group grows, so you can judge roughly where you will land and what to ask for.

Private group tour cost in Lisbon: how I work it out

I run Lisbon Sintra Tours and I have driven and guided these roads for years. When I put a quote together, it is made of a few separate lines:

  • Vehicles and drivers. This is usually the largest part, and it depends more on your route than on your numbers.
  • Guides. One guide can only talk to so many people at once, so larger groups need more than one.
  • Hours. A Lisbon and Sintra day is long. Allow a full hour each way between the two, whichever end you start from, and that time is part of the day you pay for.
  • Entry tickets. These are quoted separately, so you can see exactly what goes to the monuments and what goes to transport and guiding.

Keeping these apart makes it easier to compare my quote with anyone else's, and easier for you to explain the figure to whoever signs it off.

A group of 20

Twenty is a comfortable size. It is small enough to move through a monument as one group with one guide, and small enough to fit in vehicles that can manage the road up to Pena Palace. For many groups of this size the day feels much like a private tour for a family, just with more people.

Because the fixed costs of a vehicle and a guide are shared among fewer people, the cost per head tends to be higher than for a larger group. The total is usually the most manageable of the three sizes, though.

A group of 40

At 40, the planning changes. A single guide talking to 40 people in a palace room does not work well, so I would normally split the group in two, each with its own guide. That adds a guide to the cost, but it is what makes the day worth having.

Transport is where people are often surprised. Forty people would fit on one large coach in most cities, but the road up to Pena will not take a full-size coach. If Pena is on your list, the group needs more, smaller vehicles instead. That costs more than one big coach would, and it is worth knowing before you compare quotes. A quote that shows one large coach for a Pena day has missed something.

A group of 70

Seventy people is a proper operation. You are looking at several smaller vehicles running together, several guides, and a timetable that has to be built around timed entry rather than around a single group walking through.

Pena Palace and Quinta da Regaleira both use timed entry and both sell out. With 70 people you are likely to need several entry slots, and the groups will be staggered. That shapes the whole day: one group may be at Regaleira while another is at Pena, and the vehicles and guides move with them. This is where a day can go wrong if it is not planned properly, and it is the main thing I spend time on when I quote a large group.

The cost per head can come down at this size, because some planning and coordination is shared. It does not fall as far as people expect, because the vehicle count and the number of guides both rise with the group.

What is in a quote, and what is not

A quote from me sets out:

  • The vehicles and drivers for the day
  • The number of guides, and how the group would be split
  • The hours, from pick-up to drop-off
  • A suggested route and running order
  • Entry tickets, as a separate line, if you want me to arrange them

Lunch is not part of the standard quote. Some groups want it arranged, others prefer to leave people free. Tell me which you want and I will set out the options.

Things that change the plan, and the price

The day of the week. Jerónimos Monastery and Belém Tower are closed on Mondays. If your group is in Lisbon on a Monday and Belém matters to you, it needs to go on another day, or the Monday becomes a Sintra day.

How early you book. Because Pena and Regaleira sell out, the dates you can have depend partly on when you ask. With a large group, the earlier I know your dates, the more slots there are to choose from. I cannot guarantee availability for any particular time, but an early start gives us the most room.

How much you try to fit in. With a full hour each way between Lisbon and Sintra, a day that tries to cover both Belém and two Sintra palaces gets long, and longer days cost more. Often the better answer for a big group is fewer stops done properly.

Where you are staying. If the group is spread across several hotels, pick-ups take time and may need extra vehicle runs. One meeting point keeps the day simpler and the cost lower.

Getting a figure for your group

If you send me your dates, the number of people, and the places you most want to see, I will come back with a quote broken down along the lines above, so you can see what each part costs. If your dates are still loose, tell me that too, and I can tell you which options make the most sense for the numbers you have.
