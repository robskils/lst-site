For weddings

Everybody there, on time, and home again.

Wedding transport is the one job where arriving late is not a disappointment, it is a disaster. It is also, almost always, the last thing anybody books.

The three journeys

Most weddings here need all three.

Guests to the ceremony. Usually from two or three hotels in Lisbon to a quinta in Sintra or on the Cascais coast. The whole job is arriving together, twenty minutes early, with nobody still at the second hotel.

The day before, or the day after. Sixty guests with a free afternoon in a country most of them do not know. A guided day out is the kindest thing you can put in a welcome bag, and it keeps everybody in one place and out of trouble.

Home at the end. The one that goes wrong. A single late coach at one in the morning leaves people stranded at a quinta twenty minutes from the nearest taxi, so we run staggered departures - one at eleven for the families, one at one, one at the very end.

What we have learned

Things worth deciding early.

  • Pick-up from two hotels, not five. Each extra stop is fifteen minutes and one more chance for somebody to be in the shower. Two points, and a walk for the rest, beats five.
  • Build in half an hour you do not need. The coach that leaves at four for a five o'clock ceremony arrives at twenty to five and everybody relaxes. The one that leaves at half four has no margin at all.
  • A quinta drive is not a coach drive. Several of the loveliest venues around Sintra are up lanes a full-size coach cannot enter. We will tell you which, and bring smaller vehicles instead - two twenty-two seaters rather than one fifty.
  • Name a point of contact who is not the couple. On the day, nobody should be ringing the bride about a coach. Give us a best man, a planner, a sister - anybody with a phone and a list.
  • Late departures need to be booked, not hoped for. Drivers have legal hours. A coach that collects at four in the afternoon cannot also take everybody home at two in the morning; that is a second driver, and it has to be arranged in advance.
If the guests want a day out

Sixty guests is three vehicles and three guides.

The same arithmetic as any large group. Worth knowing before the welcome email goes out and eighty people reply yes to an afternoon in Sintra.
