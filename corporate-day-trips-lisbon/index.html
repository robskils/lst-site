For conference and incentive groups

A day out that runs to time.

A delegate day is judged on two things: whether the coach left when it said it would, and whether anybody was left behind. Everything else is the pleasant part.

What we are usually asked for

The three shapes a corporate day takes.

The half day between sessions. Thirty to sixty delegates, out at two, back by six-thirty for a dinner nobody can be late for. Usually Lisbon and Belém, because it is the one that is genuinely reliable against a clock.

The full day after the conference. Sintra, which is the better day by some distance, and which needs the whole eight hours because two of them are the road.

The incentive day. Smaller, usually, and the one where the budget is real - a winery, a private room for lunch, a boat on the river at the end. Tell us what the trip is rewarding and we will build around it.

What matters on the day

The parts that decide whether it went well.

  • A guide in every vehicle. Not one guide on a microphone in the lead coach. Three coaches means three guides, and the group in the third one has the same day as the first.
  • A hard return time, planned backwards. If they must be back at six-thirty, the day is built from six-thirty outwards, with the last stop chosen because it is on the way home.
  • Lunch that can seat the whole group at once. Sixty people arriving at a restaurant without a set menu agreed is ninety minutes, not sixty, and somebody always ends up eating alone at the bar.
  • A register, taken by the guide. It sounds like school. It is the reason nobody is left at Cabo da Roca.
  • One invoice, with a NIF on it. Issued properly for your finance team, before or after the day, in euros, with everything itemised. No per-head cash on the coach.
Planning against a clock

Two of the eight hours are the road.

This is the single most useful thing to know when an agenda has to hold. Between Lisbon and Sintra we allow a full hour each way - and a group of sixty takes ten to fifteen minutes to board, every time, which is another half hour across a day with four stops in it.

The commercial part

How we quote, and what is in it.

One figure for the day, covering the vehicles, the licensed guides, fuel, tolls, parking and the driver's hours. Entry tickets are quoted separately and at cost, because they change by site and by age band and we would rather show you the real number than bury a margin in it.

Deposit to hold the date, balance before the day. We issue a proper invoice with our tax number on it; recibos verdes or a company invoice, whichever your finance team needs.

If the final headcount moves, tell us as early as you can. Between twenty-three and forty-four people the price barely moves; crossing either of those lines changes the vehicle count, and that is where a quote changes.
