Can a group of 50 see both Pena Palace and Quinta da Regaleira in one day from Lisbon? Usually yes, but not as one group moving together, and not with much else added. Both sites sell entry in timed slots, and those slots sell out, so a large group goes in as several smaller parties at staggered times. If you plan for that from the start, the day works. If you plan as if 50 people can turn up at the gate together, the plan falls apart early in the morning.

Why timed entry changes everything for 50 people

For a couple, a sold-out slot means waiting for the next one. For 50 people it can change the order of the whole day. Pena and Regaleira both run timed entry, and the popular slots go, sometimes well ahead in busy months. Fifty tickets in a single slot is a lot to ask, so I plan on splitting the group across consecutive slots.

That has knock-on effects. If the first party enters Pena at one time and the last party some while later, the last party also leaves later, eats later and reaches Regaleira later. Every stop after the first inherits that gap. The schedule has to be built around the slots you can actually get, not the other way round.

Tickets are quoted separately from the tour. I start looking at slots as soon as I have your date and numbers, but I can't tell you a day is secure until the tickets are booked. That's the main reason I ask large groups to get in touch early.

Your coach will not get to the top

I've driven and guided the Sintra roads for years, and the road up to Pena will not take a full-size coach. It is narrow and winding, and there is no getting round that with a bigger vehicle or a better driver. A group of 50 travels in more, smaller vehicles instead.

That suits the split. Each vehicle carries one party, with its own driver, to its own entry slot. It does mean more vehicles and more drivers to coordinate, which shows in the quote. It also means the group is rarely all in one place until later in the day. If being together matters, for a company day or a family gathering, tell me and we can plan a point where everyone meets.

An honest Sintra large group itinerary

This is the shape I'd suggest for 50 people who want both palaces. Exact times depend on the slots, so read it as an order of events rather than a timetable.

  • Leave Lisbon early. Allow a full hour to Sintra. An early start puts the earlier slots within reach, and those are often the first to go.
  • Pena Palace, in waves. Each vehicle takes its party up for its slot. The palace sits high in a large park on a steep hill, with a fair amount of walking, so this is not a quick photo stop.
  • Down into Sintra town. Parties arrive at different times, which helps at lunch. Fifty people spread out are easier to feed than fifty at once. Your guides can suggest places that cope with numbers, or we can talk about a single sit-down meal if you want one.
  • Quinta da Regaleira, in waves. The same pattern again, each party on its own slot. Regaleira has narrow paths, tunnels and the well, so smaller parties suit it anyway.
  • Back to Lisbon. Another full hour, and leave margin for traffic at the end of the day. If people have evening plans, tell me so the return can allow for them.

What does not fit

With both palaces booked for a group this size, the day is full. These are the additions people most often ask about, and my honest view of each:

  • Cabo da Roca and Cascais. Good on a smaller day. With two timed palaces and 50 people, adding the coast usually means rushing a palace. If the coast matters to you, drop one of the palaces to make room.
  • Belém on the same day. Not realistic alongside Sintra for a group this size. It deserves its own day or half-day.
  • The Moorish Castle, Monserrate or the National Palace in town. All worth seeing, but each is another entry and more time. I'd swap one in for a palace rather than stack it on top.

Decisions that make the day work

  • One palace or two. If some of your group find hills and steps hard, one palace done properly, with an easier afternoon in town, can be the better day. Pena on its own with a slower afternoon is a fair choice, not a lesser one.
  • Which day of the week. If you're also planning a Lisbon day, Jerónimos and Belém Tower are closed on Mondays. Putting Sintra on the Monday and Belém on another day avoids losing a sightseeing day.
  • How far ahead to book. The earlier I have your date, the wider the choice of slots. Booking late doesn't always mean missing out, but it narrows the options.
  • Together or in parties. Decide whether the group needs to share the experience or simply needs to see the same places. The answer shapes how tightly the slots are grouped and where we regroup.

What I need from you

Your date or dates, the number of people, where you're staying in Lisbon, and whether anyone has mobility concerns. From that I can tell you what's realistic, how many vehicles it takes and what the day would cost, with entry tickets quoted separately.

If you're weighing up Sintra for a large group, send me your dates and numbers and I'll come back to you with a quote and a plan that fits.
