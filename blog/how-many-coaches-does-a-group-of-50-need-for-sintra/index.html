How Many Coaches Does a Group of 50 Need for Sintra?

A group of 50 needs two medium-sized coaches for Sintra, not one large one. The road up into Sintra's hills will not take a full-size 50-seat coach, so the group has to travel in smaller vehicles. That is a limit set by the road, not by us.

A group of 50 needs two medium-sized coaches for Sintra, not one large one. The road up into Sintra's hills will not take a full-size 50-seat coach, so the group has to travel in smaller vehicles. That is a limit set by the road, not by us. The rest of this post explains why, what the alternatives look like, and why two coaches is usually the cheaper and calmer way to do the day once you count everything.

Why a 50-seat coach can't get up to Sintra

I have driven these roads for years, and the problem is simple. The routes up to the palaces are narrow and steep, with tight bends between old stone walls and very little room to pass. A full-size coach is too long and too wide for them. Large coaches are kept off the upper roads, and even where one could squeeze through, it would block the road for everyone else.

So when you ask whether a 50-seat coach can do Sintra, the honest answer is no. The coach is fine on the motorway from Lisbon. It is the last stretch, the part you actually came for, that it cannot manage.

The option you may be offered instead

Some quotes get around this by using one big coach to bring the group to the edge of town, then moving everyone onto something else for the climb, or onto their own feet. On paper it looks like one vehicle and one price. In practice it means:

  • Your group gets split anyway. Fifty people do not move onto local transport as one unit. You end up in batches, waiting for each other.
  • Time goes on waiting rather than seeing. Every transfer is a queue, a headcount and a wait for the last few to arrive.
  • Timed tickets get harder to keep. If your entry slot at Pena is fixed and half the group is still at the bottom, the slot does not wait for you.
  • It is hard on anyone who struggles with hills. Sintra is steep. A group of 50 almost always includes somebody who would rather not walk the climb.

None of that shows up in the price, but all of it shows up on the day.

Why two coaches is not a markup

I understand the suspicion. You asked for one coach and the quote says two. It is fair to wonder whether someone is padding the bill.

Here is where the money goes. Two vehicles means two drivers, and a medium coach does not cost half of a large one to run. So yes, two medium coaches cost more than one large coach would if one large coach could do the job. But it cannot, so that is not the real comparison. The real comparison is two coaches that go door to door against one coach plus transfers, extra time and the risk of missing your entry slots.

Two coaches also give you things a single vehicle never could:

  • Flexibility on timing. The two halves of the group can visit the palaces in a different order, which makes timed entry much easier to arrange.
  • Smaller groups at each stop. Twenty-five people around a guide can hear and see. Fifty cannot.
  • A choice of pace. If some of the group want a gentler day, one coach can follow a lighter plan without holding the other back.

What a large group tour of Sintra by coach actually looks like

The most important thing to know about a large group tour in Sintra is that the coach is the easy part. Tickets are the hard part.

Pena Palace and Quinta da Regaleira both use timed entry, and both sell out. Finding 50 places in a single slot is difficult, especially in busy months, and leaving it late can mean there is no slot at all. Splitting the group across two coaches helps here as well. One half can start at Pena while the other starts at Regaleira, then they swap. Each half needs fewer tickets per slot, which is far easier to secure.

Entry tickets are quoted separately from transport and guiding, so you can see exactly what you are paying for each part. If you already know which sites you want, tell us early and we will look at what is available for your dates.

If you are adding Lisbon or Belém

Many groups pair Sintra with a day in Lisbon. If Belém is on your list, plan around Mondays. The Jerónimos Monastery and Belém Tower are both closed on Mondays, so a Monday is a better day for Sintra than for Belém.

The two coaches work well in Lisbon too. The group stays in the same vehicles all day with the same drivers, and nobody has to change transport halfway through.

A quick guide for other group sizes

The number of vehicles follows the number of people, so count everyone. That means teachers, organisers and anyone else travelling with the group, not just the paying guests. A group that is 48 on the booking form is often 52 by the time the leaders are added, and that can change what you need.

As a rough rule, a group small enough for one medium coach can travel together. Above that, you need two, and much larger groups need three or more. The exact split depends on the vehicles available for your dates, which is why I would rather quote from your real numbers than guess.
